# The Praesidium data taxonomy and the praesidium document

Status: r2, 2026-11-01, draft for Daedalus review (T-12244). **Not for implementation: Lance is weighing whether the change is worth making.** r2 is written under three rulings Lance gave for the purpose of closing the design (hosted inside rusthrc; remote seats in revision one; M5 approved) and closes every assumption r1.2 left open, from source and live reads of 2026-11-01 ~17:45Z. r1.2 was approved by Daedalus as a design (EN-40711). Owner mable@hrc-runtime:primary. Rulings by Lance are marked **RULED**. Diagrams: `diagrams/taxonomy.arris-board.json#taxonomy-subjects`, `diagrams/seat-data.arris-board.json`.

## 0. Claim

Praesidium's data types grew organically in three systems over a year: wrkq (Go, the shared ledger: tasks, conversations, obligations), HRC (Rust, execution: seats, generations, runtimes) and agent-spaces (agents, as profile directories). Each names its records after itself (`hrc.seat`, `wrkq.task`, `aspd.compile`), each has its own escape hatch for client-owned data (`seat_annotations` in HRC; `tasks.meta` keys by convention in wrkq), and a reader that wants "this seat, its agent, its task, its workstream, its pending handoff" joins three stores by hand with keys spelled four ways.

This record does two things:

1. **A taxonomy.** Every record is classified by its **subject** (what it is about: one of nine) and its **register** (what kind of truth it holds: one of five). A record lives with the system that mints its subject's key; a record about two subjects lives with the one whose lifecycle ends it. Type names are `subject[.kind]`; the minting system is the *provider*, not part of the name.
2. **One document.** Readers mount one cardo document, `praesidium`, hosted inside rusthrc beside the `hrc` document it will replace, that projects every subject from every system under taxonomy names. The write side is unchanged: HRC, wrkq and agent-spaces keep their stores, APIs, fences and authority. **The read model is unified; the write models are not merged.**

**RULED (Lance, 2026-11-01):** the separation into three systems was made for ownership boundaries; those pay on the write side and never bought a reader anything — unify the document. Handoffs belong to the seat and move to HRC, durable. Agents stay in `var/agents` and are represented, not moved. hcs is renamed **workstream**; a seat decides when its thread becomes one. c2 is Lance's own workflow; a seat's open questions are a generic capability (`seat.asks`, T-12239). For r2: the document is **hosted inside rusthrc**; **remote seats are in revision one**; **M5 (the wrkq extension register) is approved** as a wrkq contract change. Whether to build at all remains open (§7).

## 2. The taxonomy

### 2.1 Subjects

| subject | key | minted by | identity today |
|---|---|---|---|
| **agent** | `agent:<id>` | agent-spaces (`var/agents/<id>/` exists; `archived-agents/<id>/` retired) | `agent-profile.toml [identity]` |
| **project** | `projectId` (slug) · P- | wrkq | `containers kind=project`; mirrored `hrc.project` |
| **work group** | P- / A- | wrkq | `containers kind=directory\|feature\|area`; a campaign is a feature + its room |
| **task** | T- | wrkq | `tasks` kind task \| subtask \| bug \| chore \| spike |
| **workstream** | T- (`kind=workstream`) | wrkq | today an hcs task + `meta.hcs_*` |
| **room** | R- | wrkq | `rooms` kind task \| campaign \| project \| adhoc; members are seats |
| **seat** | `scopeRef` = agent × project × lane | HRC | `hrc.seat`; **kind ∈ {task, standing, adhoc}** (new, §2.3 R6); the join of all three systems |
| **generation** | `hostSessionId` | HRC | `hrc.generation`, N of a seat |
| **runtime** | `runtimeId` | HRC | `hrc.runtime` |

A thing is a subject when other records are about it and it has its own lifecycle. A promise is not a subject (nothing is about a promise): it is an obligation record owned by a principal. Its subject is the task or container it references; a promise that references neither (`wrkq/internal/domain/promises.go`: a principal-owned text subject; PR-00006 open on 2026-11-01, 10 of 198 retained) is the owner's own commitment and is placed on the **agent** row.

### 2.2 Registers

| register | meaning | write discipline | ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| **identity** | the thing exists; this is its key | written once by the minting system | retired, never edited |
| **state** | the current truth, one value at a time | overwritten in place and superseded; **whether a write is fenced is a per-type declaration** (`writable: fenced` with `etag` / `expect.updatedAt`, or `writable: unfenced`, last-writer-wins), never implied by the register. Session metadata is `unfenced` for every writer, launch and `PATCH /v1/sessions/metadata` alike, under `hrc-runtime.session-identity-metadata`, unchanged; annotations and tasks are `fenced`; every new state type in this record (`seat.asks`, `seat.work`, `seat.handoff`) is `fenced` by its own contract | superseded |
| **obligation** | owed from A to B, open until discharged | two parties, a lifecycle, a deadline or trigger; someone can be late | discharged, withdrawn, failed |
| **ledger** | what happened | appended, never changed; ordered; retention is a policy | never (keep-forever by default in this fleet) |
| **extension** | an opaque body a third party attaches to a subject it does not own | `{subject, ns, kind, id, body, fence}`; the writer owns the schema; the ns names it | with its subject, or by its writer |

The register fixes the **shape** of the write API: state is set and superseded, ledger is appended and read by cursor, obligation is created and discharged and can be late, extension is put under a namespace. It fixes no fence. Fencing is declared per type (`writable: fenced | unfenced | none`) from the owner's active contract, so a reader learns whether it has a fence to supply by reading the declaration, and no owner API changes to satisfy the taxonomy (D1). The one unfenced state type today is session metadata; the record does not propose to fence it.

### 2.3 Rules

- **R1 Minting.** A record lives with the system that mints its subject's key. A record about two subjects lives with the one whose lifecycle ends it.
- **R1a Obligations between principals.** An obligation owed between parties (envelope, promise, workflow obligation) lives in the collaboration ledger, wrkq, whatever subject it is about, because wrkq owns the obligation register between parties. An obligation internal to one subject's lifecycle (a handoff between two generations of one seat; a claim on a task) lives with that subject's minter.
- **R2 Naming.** Type names are `subject[.kind]`: `seat`, `seat.asks`, `seat.handoff`, `task`, `task.comment`, `room.envelope`, `agent`. The provider (who declares and serves the type) is recorded beside the name — in cardo, literally the `provider` field `registry.discover` returns per type. `hrc.*` and `aspd.*` are legacy names retired when readers have moved to the praesidium document. Because the registry is host-wide, the bare names are reserved for the host once declared.
- **R2a Declared attributes.** Register, minting system, writability and window are carried on every type as `capabilities` strings — `register:state`, `minted:wrkq`, `writable:fenced`, `window:open+24h` — because cardo's declaration schemas are closed and `capabilities` is the one free-form, discoverable field. A reader classifies a type by reading them; the record's grid is the source they are generated from.
- **R3 Durability vs projection.** Keeping is a store property; projecting is a window. Every store keeps forever unless a retention rule is approved; the document shows only the current situation per subject (identity and state as nodes; obligations as nodes until discharged plus a short window; extensions as opaque nodes; ledger never, except as derived "latest" fields).
- **R4 Canonical keys.** In every payload: the full `scopeRef` for seats (`sessionRef` only where the lane matters), the public id (`T-`, `P-`, `R-`, `EN-`, `PR-`) for wrkq subjects, `agent:<id>` for agents. wrkq's collaboration tables store the **handle** form; the projector normalises handle → full at projection (`hrc-common::parse_scope_handle`) and carries the uuid → public id map it builds at bootstrap, since event payloads are keyed by uuid. Handles and uuids are display and storage forms, never join keys.
- **R5 Authority after a move.** When a record moves between systems, the record names the commit from which the new home is authoritative; the old rows become history and are never projected.
- **R6 Seat kind.** `kind = task` when the task segment is `T-<digits>`; `kind = standing` when the task segment (or its slot base, `-nova…-cosmos` stripped) is a key of the agent's `placement.homes` or the task part of one of its `placement.pins` and is not a `T-` id; `kind = adhoc` otherwise. Computed at seat creation from the same `resolveRuntimeDeclaration` the summon gate already runs, stored on the continuity, re-evaluated on profile change. Today's rule-by-string sites (`seat_retirement.rs`, `shutdown-intent`) read the field instead.

## 3. The praesidium document

### 3.1 One document, three providers, one mount

Document `praesidium` is opened by rusthrc in the same `CoreAdapter` and on the same socket as `hrc` (`document.open {document_id:"praesidium", rebuildable, ingest_only, durability:none}`), so Loggia's address is `hrc:praesidium` with no new `kernels` entry. Three providers are enabled, one per feed, because a provider has one worker behind one mutex and `LedgerIngest` answers one operation: `praesidium.hrc` (declares `seat`, `seat.generation`, `seat.runtime`, `seat.asks`, `seat.handoff`, `seat.ext`, `project` mirror), `praesidium.wrkq` (declares `project`, `work.group`, `task`, `workstream`, `room`, `room.envelope`, `promise`, `task.workflow`, `ext`) and `praesidium.agents` (declares `agent`). Readers see one document; `registry.discover` shows the provider per type, which is R2's "provider beside the name". Every type is an observation type with a natural key and a `source` attribute (ingest requirement), carries R2a `capabilities`, and declares `live_until`. A fourth type, `feed`, keyed by name, is written by every binding (§3.8). **No hierarchy is declared in revision one** (§3.7).

rusthrc changes, all in the kernel host, none in HRC's write paths: a `declarations()` per provider; a builder registering each `in_process_worker` and its `LedgerIngest`; two more `registry.enable`, one more `document.open`, one `ingest.attach` per binding; wake wiring per binding (`kernel/mod.rs:157–165`); `watch_errors`/`binding_error` over every document and binding; `Kernel.tasks` as a `Vec`; tests that hardcode `"hrc"`. The `hrc` document stays open and unchanged until readers have moved (§9).

### 3.2 Feeds

| binding | provider | source | cursor | catch-up | projects |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `hrc` | praesidium.hrc | the existing `HrcLedger` + `SeatProjector`, re-targeted to taxonomy names and extended with `kind`, `work`, `seat.asks`, `seat.handoff` | `hrc_seq` fenced by `ledgerIncarnationId`, as today | as today | this node's seats, generations, runtimes, seat records |
| `hrc@<peer>` (one per peer in `FederationConfig.peers`) | praesidium.hrc | **poll** of peer `GET /v1/sessions/page?nodes=local` (fingerprinted; unchanged pages cost nothing) joined with `GET /v1/federation/runtimes` (per-node `state`, `answeredAt`, last-good cache) | the page fingerprint + the runtimes `answeredAt` | caught up after the first full page walk, including an empty one when the peer is unreachable (§3.8) | remote seats with `node`, status, generation, runtime status; **no remote attention, events or annotations in revision one** |

**wrkq bootstrap, sized against today:** `project.listView` (111 projects) → `task.list {path: <project>, recursive, state: [open, in_progress, blocked, draft]}` paged at 500 (396 live tasks + 34 draft; `meta` arrives with the row); `container.list` per project for groups (673); `room.list {all}` once (every room is hydrated, ~2,212 rows — accepted at bootstrap, filtered to the 7-day window locally); and **three read views wrkq does not have today and this record adds under M5's contract change**, all bounded and read-only: `envelope.listView {states, limit, cursor}` (the store already supports `States`/`ToScopeRefs`; `pendingView` cannot be used because it is per addressee and runs sweeps), `promise.listView {states, limit, cursor}` (today per owner), `workflow.instancesView {statuses, limit, cursor}` (today per task). Until they exist the feed carries no envelopes, promises or instances; with them bootstrap is ≈ 1,600 nodes. **Replay:** a delta row names a uuid; the projector re-reads the subject through the show call for its noun (`task.show`, `container.show`, `room.show`, `envelope.show`, `promise.show`) and upserts the whole node, so a node is never assembled from deltas. The feed owns its **own `ledger::Client`** (the shared one is one child behind one mutex at 15 s per call) and authenticates with the node token; the read views need no principal.

**Agents:** the `agent` node carries `agentId`, `status`, `identity {role, operator}`, `placement {launch, pins, homes}`, `provisioning.scalars`, `profileHash` (= `source.agentProfile.contentHash`), `sourcePath`. `display` and `avatar` are not returned by the resolve read and are read from the TOML by the scan (they are validated there, `profile.rs:256–283`); `spaces`, `session` and `instructions` are not projected. Archived agents are a new scan of `archived-agents/`; raspd gains no new op.

### 3.3 Cross-node seats — in revision one (RULED)

A remote seat is projected by its node's `hrc@<peer>` binding from the peer's own session page, with `node` on the node and the binding's `source` stamp, so sweeps and resets are per peer. What the page carries is what revision one shows: identity, status, active generation and runtime status; attention, annotations and events stay node-local until a peer events page exists (not in revision one, §6). Operations on a remote seat route to its home exactly as today (`federation/routing.rs`). **Contract impact:** `hrc-runtime.federated-session-read` limits federation to scope-home single-scope reads and forbids fan-out; this binding is a read-only, bounded, per-peer page poll under the existing peer allowlist and bearer auth, and the invariant is amended to admit exactly that — the amendment is part of this record's adoption, not a separate ruling.

### 3.4 Operations

The providers declare the operations readers invoke and delegate each to its owner: HRC operations (`summon`, `send`, `seat.annotation.put/act`, `handoff.acknowledge`) by in-process owner dispatch as today; wrkq operations (`task.create`, `room.say`, `ext.put`) through the existing `ledger::Client`; nothing for agents (edit the profile). Fences are the owner's; the providers add none. The document is `ingest_only`, so a non-ingest commit refuses `projection_read_only` by construction. Write authority does not move.

### 3.5 Hosting — inside rusthrc (RULED)

Hosted in rusthrc's kernel on the owner daemon, as the `hrc` document is. The dependency-direction concern r1 raised is void: rusthrc already reads and writes wrkq at runtime through `Server.wrkq` (five production calls) and reads aspd through `AspSession`. The feeds are optional and degradable (§3.8): an unreachable wrkqd or aspd never stops the kernel starting or the `hrc` binding serving.

### 3.6 Live windows

`live_until` is computed per upsert by the projector; a window change is a projector constant, not a schema change. Revision one: seat, generation, runtime — as today (generation: runtime gone + 24 h); `seat.handoff` — pending, plus 24 h after acknowledge; `seat.asks`, `seat.work`, `seat.ext` — with the seat; agent — all; project — all; work group — unarchived; task — state ∈ {open, in_progress, blocked, draft} (396 + 34 today) plus any task a live seat's `work` names, 24 h after leaving those states; workstream — open; room — `last_activity_at` within 7 days (346 today); `room.envelope` — pending or presented, gone on terminal (103 today; **metadata only: id, room, from, to, obligation, state, task, createdAt — no body**); promise — open (20); `task.workflow` — active or waiting (38); `ext` — with its subject. Bootstrap ≈ 1,600 wrkq nodes + 50 agents + the seats as today.

### 3.7 Lineage

Hierarchies (`agent ⊃ seat ⊃ generation ⊃ runtime`, `project ⊃ work group ⊃ task`, `agent ⊃ workstream`) are **not declared in revision one**: cardo's registry is host-wide, a declared hierarchy applies to every node, and the live `hrc.*` nodes would fail their next upsert with `hierarchy_missing`. They are declared in the step where the `hrc` document retires (§9 step 5), after the cardo pin moves to ≥ `4404357` (T-12184 keyed-parent upsert). Until then every edge is a payload key (R4). When declared: parents must be live before children in the same batch, so the agents binding bootstraps first and the hrc binding orders a seat after its agent; a cross-feed parent that is not live backs the child's binding off rather than failing the document.

### 3.8 Degradation: stale, never absent

Each binding writes one `feed` node per batch — `{name, cursor, cursorAt, state: live | stale | unreachable, lastError?}` where `cursorAt` is the source row's own time (the last event's timestamp; the page's `answeredAt`; the newest profile mtime), never "now", so an idle feed writes nothing. Readers compute age against their clock. Three rules keep the three-system failure isolation visible:

1. **A binding that cannot reach its source at bootstrap reports `caught_up` with an empty snapshot** and a `feed` row `unreachable`, so the document's `bootstrapping` flag (the AND of every binding) clears and the `hrc` binding's seats are served. It retries on its tick and re-bootstraps when the source answers. All bindings declare `reports_catch_up`.
2. **A binding whose source stops answering after bootstrap keeps its nodes** and marks its `feed` row `stale`; `live_until` sweeps continue on the kernel clock, so stale nodes still age out of their windows.
3. **A reader that needs authority asks the owner**, never the document: an operation routes to the owner and carries the owner's fence.

Transition risk, named: the subscriber queue is shared across documents, so while Loggia subscribes to `hrc` only, a `praesidium` bootstrap can overflow that subscriber (`lag`, reconnect, resync). Bootstrap batches are therefore bounded (≤ 1000 edits per call, as cardo enforces) and spaced on the binding's interval; Loggia's existing resync path covers an overflow.

### 3.9 Rebuild

The document is `durability: none`; every daemon restart re-bootstraps every binding from its source (as `hrc` does today). The wrkq bootstrap is ≈ 1,600 nodes over ≈ 150 RPC calls; the agents bootstrap is 50 resolves; the peer bootstrap is one page walk per peer. A rebuild is therefore a restart, and gate 3 measures it.

## 5. Decisions

| id | decision | why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Unify the document, not the systems. | The boundaries pay on the write side; a reader never benefited from three documents; lineage across systems needs one. |
| D2 | Taxonomy names from day one on the new providers; legacy `hrc.*` retired by mount switch, not rename. | The `hrc` document simply stops being mounted; plugin edits are the ~10 hardcoded address/type sites, done once. |
| D3 | Register, minting system, writability (`writable: fenced \| unfenced \| none`) and window are declared per type as `capabilities` strings (R2a). | Discoverable through `registry.discover` with no kernel change; writability is read from each owner's active contract, never from the register, so no existing API changes (F2). |
| D4 | Stale, never absent; one `feed` node per binding with the source's own time; empty bootstrap on an unreachable source. | Keeps three-system failure isolation visible and keeps the document's `bootstrapping` flag honest. |
| D5 | Durability is the store's; projection is a window (R3). | Handoffs, envelopes, workstreams are kept forever and projected while live. |
| D6 | Canonical keys (R4) ruled before any projector is written; handle → full at projection. | The cheapest decision and the one that bites daily if skipped; wrkq stores both forms today. |
| D7 | `seat.kind` by R6; `seat.work` late-bound; promote-by-default under the lane name with merge/rename on the board. | Lance never types a task or workstream name before talking to a seat; the seat decides when its thread becomes a workstream; the backstop asks only when the turn made work real. |
| D8 | wrkq's extension register has the same shape as HRC's annotation. | One shape across the fleet; one filter in a pane. |
| D9 | Three providers under one document, one per feed. | A provider's worker serializes its calls behind one mutex; a slow wrkq page must not hold the hrc feed. Readers see one document either way. |
| D10 | No hierarchy in revision one; lineage declared when the `hrc` document retires, after the cardo pin bump. | A hierarchy is host-wide and would break live `hrc.*` upserts. |
| D11 | Remote seats from a per-peer session-page poll, not a peer events feed. | The page and the runtimes probe exist under the peer allowlist today; an events page and remote-state projector do not, and attention is not needed to show a seat exists and is busy. |
| D12 | Three bounded read views added to wrkq (envelopes by state, promises by state, instances by status). | Without them the feed cannot bootstrap obligations without per-addressee sweeps (`pendingView` writes) or per-task walks. |
| D13 | Replay re-reads the subject; nodes are never assembled from deltas. | `event_log` payloads are partial and uuid-keyed. |
| D14 | Envelope nodes carry metadata only. | Bodies are read through `wrkc show`; 103 live envelopes stay small in every subscriber's resync. |

## 6. Explicitly unsupported

Merging the daemons or stores; moving write authority or fences into the providers; HRC validating wrkq or agent bodies; projecting any ledger (comments, events, transitions, `project_events`) as nodes; projecting closed tasks, terminal envelopes, archived containers or envelope bodies; push or subscribe from wrkq (`bounded-polling-streaming`); `monitor.eventsView` as a feed (it filters and writes); remote attention, annotations or events in revision one (needs a peer `/v1/events/page` and a remote-state projector — later, under its own invariant amendment); hierarchies while the `hrc` document is live; inferring `seat.work` from conversation text or from provenance alone; inferring a legacy handoff's seat from its text (hand assignment at cutover); fencing session metadata writes, launch or API; renaming wrkq RPC nouns; an agent store outside `var/agents`; a raspd list-agents op (the scan suffices); per-field history in the document; persisting the document or its cursors (a restart is a rebuild).

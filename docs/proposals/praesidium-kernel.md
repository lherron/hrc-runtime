# The praesidium kernel: one surface for every agent

Status: r1.3, 2026-11-01; r1.2 was **APPROVED by Daedalus as a design** (EN-40730, T-12245), not adopted and not approved to build; r1.3 replaces the owner-verb carry-over with one verb grammar derived from the registers (Lance, 2026-11-01: wrkq's `cat`/`ls`/`touch` are Unix syntax, not a standard), restores the "not an operation" paragraph r1.2 dropped by mistake, and changes no structure; r1.2 resolved Daedalus F2 second cycle (EN-40727): the kernel never repeats a write; a reconciliation class says what the agent may safely do under the owner's own contract. r1.1 resolved F1 and F3 (EN-40725): HRC operations carry the daemon-minted lifecycle credential, and the node-local own store is withdrawn (ext and workstream stay in wrkq as the taxonomy record ruled). Companion to `praesidium-document.md` r2.2 (T-12244, APPROVED as a design), which it amends in two places (§4) and otherwise adopts whole. **Not for implementation.** Lance ruled 2026-11-01 that the intent is for every agent (mable, clod, cody and the rest) to migrate off the individual wrkq, wrkc and hrc surfaces onto one standard, and asked whether to derisk that by leaving the existing systems as they are and building a separate praesidium kernel. This record answers yes to the separate kernel and no to "replace them all" as a commitment: **surfaces are replaced; stores are strangled by rule.** Owner mable@hrc-runtime:primary. Facts are from source and live reads of 2026-11-01 ~21:30Z.

## 0. Claim

The taxonomy record unifies what is *read*: one cardo document, `praesidium`, projecting nine subjects from three systems under one vocabulary. It was written for one reader, Loggia, and hosted inside rusthrc on that assumption. With every agent as a consumer, two things change:

1. **The host moves out of rusthrc.** The surface every agent's tools run through must not share a process with the daemon that births, rotates and restarts those agents. It becomes its own kernel, the idiomatic cardo shape (foundry-kernel, raspd's aspd kernel, rusthrc's hrc kernel, cardo-proof each host their own), one per node, reading HRC the way the record already reads peers.
2. **The record gains the half agents use.** A document answers "what is the current situation"; an agent also writes, reads history and bodies, waits, and must be *who it is* in every ledger row. That is an operation catalog with caller identity, served as generated tools. cardo already has the mechanism: discovery generates agent tools from declarations, and `cardo-agent` serves durable tool outcomes on an `agent.sock` beside `kernel.sock`, as foundry-kernel does today.

The owners stay as they are. wrkq, HRC and agent-spaces keep their stores, APIs, fences and authority. Agents stop calling them. An old register moves only by a per-subject ruling that deletes the old path in the same campaign. The kernel holds no store of its own in revision one (r1.1, F3): a register about a shared subject lives with the system that mints the subject, because every node's consumers reach that system and would not reach a node-local store.

The CLI builds them from `HRC_SESSION_REF`, `HRC_RUNTIME_ID` and the file `<runtimeRoot>/lifecycle/<runtimeId>.credential` (`hrc-cli/src/restartme.rs:124–157`); any client in the runtime's environment can present them, and HRC does the verifying.
- **Idempotency is uneven across owners.** wrkq honors `idempotencyKey` with replay-or-conflict (`wrkqapi/api.go:104–142`, table `wrkq_rpc_idempotency`) on `task.create`, `task.update`, `comment.add`, `room.say`, `handoff.create`, `projectEvent.post`, `container.update`, `relation.add`, `workflow.attach`, `attachment.add`; `task.move`, container ops, `comment.delete` and `task.restore` take an etag fence (`expectEtag` / `ifMatch`). Identity, if any, is an argument the operation declares. `OperationDeclaration` carries `effect: read | write` ("the conservative answer for a tool generator"), `fences`, `idempotency {field, horizon_s}` and `reports_catch_up` (`cardo-core/src/registry.rs:249–300`); discovery lists every operation and `agent_tools()` generates tools from it (`docs/cardo-wire-v2.md:89, 426`; `cardo-ecosystem-contract-v1.md:71`).

## 2. Shape

### 2.1 Process

One **praesidium kernel** per node: a Rust binary in its own repo and project, a cardo host on `var/run/praesidium/kernel.sock` with a `cardo-agent` host on `var/run/praesidium/agent.sock`, state under `var/state/praesidium/` (the `praesidium.cardo` snapshot, a cache and never authority), `com.praesidium.praesidium-kernel` under launchd, in `just fleet-status` from day one. Loggia mounts it as a new `kernels` entry; the address of the document is `praesidium:praesidium`. **It is a client of HRC, wrkqd and aspd and a dependency of none of them** (S4, §3): HRC's kernel, rotation, summon and placement never call it.

### 2.2 Documents

- **`praesidium`**: the taxonomy record's document (§3 there), persisted (`explicit_save`, 60 s and on shutdown), three feeds, stale-never-absent, `feed` node per binding, no hierarchy until the `hrc` document retires. Adopted whole except the three amendments in §4.
- **No store of its own in revision one** (r1.1, F3). Extension and workstream stay in wrkq as the taxonomy record ruled (M4, M5), because their writers and consumers (scribe-cli, the ACP reconciler, taskboard's publish authorization, hcs) run on different nodes and share the canonical wrkqd today; a node-local store would partition them. A kernel store is reconsidered only with a shared store design (§6, S5).

### 2.4 The operation catalog: the standard

Operations are the standard agents migrate to; the document is the cache in front of them. Three tiers, every one declared with `effect`, `fences`, caller arguments and, for writes, `idempotency`:

1. **Read the situation**: cardo queries over the `praesidium` document by type and key, generated as read tools from discovery (`seat.list`, `task.list`, `room.members`, `feed.list`). Answered from the snapshot; carry the `feed` state so an agent can see it is reading stale.
2. **Read history and bodies**: `effect: read` operations delegated to the owner, because record R3 and §6 keep ledgers, closed tasks, terminal envelopes and bodies out of the document and that stays true. wrkq: `task.get`, `task.find`, `task.history`, `task.comment.list`, `room.history`, `room.envelope.get`, `room.envelope.find` (the inbox: pending and presented mail to the caller, bodies included), `seat.handoff.get`, `promise.get`, `promise.find`, `work.group.history`, `task.workflow.get`. HRC: `seat.get`, `seat.history` (turns, runs, events), `generation.get`, `runtime.get`, `runtime.list`, `node.get` (server status). Sweeps that the owner runs on a read (`envelope.pendingView`, `inboxView` behind `room.envelope.find`) run as they do today, under the caller's identity, because the caller is the addressee.
3. **Write**: `effect: write` operations delegated to the owner through `agent.sock` (durable admission, receipt, idempotency key = the tool call's `action_id`, as foundry). wrkq: `task.create`, `task.update`, `task.move`, `task.archive`, `task.remove`, `task.claim`, `task.release`, `task.comment.create`, `work.group.create/update`, `room.say`, `room.join/leave`, `room.envelope.defer/withdraw`, `promise.create/renew/resolve/abandon`, `task.workflow.attach`, `project.event.create`, `task.attachment.create`, `workstream.create/update`, `ext.put/delete` (subject task, work group, room; M5). HRC: `seat.summon`, `seat.send`, `seat.interrupt`, `seat.restart` (the rotation door; carries M1's handoff), `seat.update` (session metadata), `seat.asks.create/update`, `seat.handoff.create/acknowledge`, `ext.put/act/delete` (subject seat; today's annotations, same shape as M5 by D8). Waits (`wrkq monitor wait`, `hrc monitor`) are read operations with a bounded timeout over the owner's wait primitive (`monitor.stateView` polling; `events/bounded-stream`), not kernel-side loops.

**Reconciliation class (r1.1, F2; r1.2 narrowed after the second cycle).** **The kernel never repeats a write on its own.** Its `agent.sock` journal records admission and outcome; an outcome the owner answered but the kernel could not journal is reported to the agent as `uncertain {operation, action_id}` for every class, exactly as a lost CLI reply is today, and the agent decides. What a class declares is what the *agent* may safely do next, under the owner's own contract, and it is published in the tool description and the parity table, taken from the owner's declaration at build time and never assumed:

- **`keyed`**: the owner replays by idempotency key. The kernel passes the tool call's `action_id` as the owner's `idempotencyKey` on the first call; the agent may repeat with the same `action_id`, and the owner returns the recorded result or `idempotency key reused with a different request`. wrkq: `task.create`, `task.update`, `task.comment.create`, `room.say`, `project.event.create`, `work.group.update`, `task.relation.create`, `task.workflow.attach`, `task.attachment.create` (wrkq's `handoff.create` is keyed too but retires with M1). HRC: `seat.summon` (claim-and-start replays by key). **The replay guarantee is the owner's, including its window:** wrkq records the replay row in a separate insert after the effect commits (`wrkqapi/comments.go:43–93`, `api.go:135–147`), so a wrkqd interruption inside that window leaves an effect whose key the owner never recorded. That window is the same for the CLI today, is the owner's to close (cardo's wire v2 idempotency rule puts deduplication in the product transaction), and is explicitly unsupported here (§6); the kernel neither claims to cover it nor gates it.
- **`fenced`**: the owner takes an etag or revision fence on the first call. The agent repeats with the same fence; success means the write landed once, `conflict` means something landed and the current state is read to tell which. wrkq: `task.move`, `work.group.archive/move/remove/update`, `task.comment.remove`, `task.restore`. HRC: `ext.put`, `ext.delete` on a seat (today's annotation put/delete; both take `expect`, `projection/sessions.rs:443–467`), `seat.asks.*`, `seat.handoff.acknowledge`.
- **`unkeyed`**: the owner records neither, or ignores what is sent. The agent reads before deciding. HRC: `seat.send` (literal input stores the key but does not replay it, and delivers before recording), **`ext.act` on a seat** (today's annotation act: the route calls `act_annotation` without `expect` and the store appends an action event without changing the annotation, `hrc-store/src/annotations.rs:436–466`; a repeat appends a second action), `seat.interrupt`, `seat.update` (session metadata, unfenced by contract), `seat.restart`. wrkq: `room.envelope.defer/withdraw`, `promise.*`, `task.claim/release`, `room.join/leave`.

An `unkeyed` operation that agents use heavily is a candidate for an owner additive before its verbs migrate: HRC enforcing the `idempotencyKey` its literal-input table already stores (a keyed lookup in `active_input_deliveries` before delivery) would move `seat.send` to `keyed`. Named here as the one such additive worth proposing; until it lands `seat.send` is `unkeyed` and the tool says so.

**Naming (r1.3).** An operation is `type.verb`, where the type is the taxonomy's `subject[.kind]` (record R2) and the verb comes from **one grammar derived from the registers**, never from an owner's CLI or RPC spelling. wrkq's `cat`, `ls`, `touch`, `set`, `mv`, `rm`, `tree` and `log` are Unix file metaphors that stop at its CLI; its RPC names (`task.catView`, `task.findListView`, `envelope.inboxView`) are view names that stop at its wire. The parity table maps each of them to a grammar verb; the mapping is the table's job, not the name's.

| register | verbs | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| any | `get` · `list` · `find` | `get {key}` reads one record from its **owner**, bodies and fence included (today's `cat` / `show`); `list {filter}` reads many from the **document**, current situation only; `find {query, state}` searches the owner, closed and terminal included |
| identity | `create` · `archive` · `remove` · `move` | mint; retire; soft-delete; re-parent |
| state | `update {expectEtag}` | one fenced write of the fields named; no separate `set`, `patch` or `edit` |
| obligation | `say` · `reply` · `defer` · `withdraw` · `acknowledge` · `resolve` · `abandon` · `renew` · `wait` | open, answer, pause, cancel, consume, satisfy, drop, extend, and block until a state with a bounded timeout |
| ledger | `history` · `create` on the ledger type | `history {key, kinds, since}` reads a subject's ledger (comments, transitions, events, room log, turns, runs); appending is `create` on the ledger type (`task.comment.create`) |
| extension | `put` · `get` · `list` · `delete` · `act` | one shape for HRC's seat annotations and wrkq's M5 extensions (D8): `ext.put {subject, ns, kind, id, body, expectEtag}` |

Subject-specific verbs are the closed set the grammar cannot express: `seat.summon`, `seat.send`, `seat.interrupt`, `seat.restart`, `task.claim`, `task.release`, `room.join`, `room.leave`, `task.workflow.attach`. Nothing else may add a verb without amending this table. There are no aliases: `inbox` is `room.envelope.find {to: "me"}` and its tool description says "your inbox"; `wrkq monitor wait` is `task.wait`; `hrc restartme` is `seat.restart`. The **parity table** (every one of the 168 CLI forms → `type.verb`, or → "human only", or → "retired", each write with its reconciliation class) is an artifact of step 1 (§9) and the subject of gate 1; the catalog is complete when the table has no blank row.

**Not an operation.** `hrc server restart/stop`, `just install`, `hrc daemon` lifecycle, `wrkq index`, webhooks, `wrkf.*`: operator and workflow-engine surfaces, human or wrkf only. Restart authority stays exactly where the restart doctrine puts it. (Restored in r1.3; dropped by an editing mistake in r1.2.)

### 2.5 Caller identity

Every operation carries the caller: `{principalRef, scopeRef}`.

- **Bound once per connection.** The bridge (§2.6) runs inside the runtime's environment and reads what the CLIs read: the principal, `HRC_SESSION_REF`, `HRC_RUNTIME_ID`, and the daemon-minted lifecycle credential at `<runtimeRoot>/lifecycle/<runtimeId>.credential` (r1.1, F1). It presents all of them in its hello on `agent.sock` and the principal and scope as a query attribute on `kernel.sock`. The kernel **refuses a hello whose scope is not a live seat in its `hrc@<this node>` binding** (`unknown_caller`), and refuses a principal that is not that seat's agent. The kernel never verifies the credential itself; HRC does.
- **Forwarded, never substituted.** To wrkqd as `X-Wrkq-Principal-Ref` plus the scope params the method takes, so every ledger row names the agent, not the kernel (gate 2). To HRC as the three lifecycle headers (`x-hrc-lifecycle-session-ref`, `-runtime-id`, `-credential`) on every HRC operation, verbatim, so `credentials::verify_caller` sees the runtime that is calling, binds it to its live scope and generation, and `seat.restart` and the lifecycle route refuse or admit exactly as they do for `hrc restartme` today. **HRC changes nothing for identity**; the r1 "caller scope on operator routes" additive is withdrawn. Trust level: wrkq operations are self-asserted as today plus the seat-table check; HRC lifecycle operations carry the daemon-minted credential as today. Neither is a new authority model.
- **`cause`.** Every command carries `{principalRef, scopeRef, taskId?, action_id}` as its cardo `cause` (wire v2 §12), so receipts and the kernel's own ledger say who and why.

### 2.6 Transport: generated tools, provisioned by agent-spaces

- **`praesidium-mcp`**, a stdio MCP bridge shipped by the kernel repo: on start it reads discovery over `kernel.sock`, generates one tool per declared operation (read tools over `kernel.sock`, write tools over `agent.sock`), and refreshes on registry change. Tool descriptions come from the declarations' `description` fields, which replace the injected `wrkq info` / `wrkc info` guides for migrated verbs.
- **Provisioned as a space.** A `praesidium-tools` space in `spaces-repo` declares the MCP server; raspd composes it into every harness home it already composes `mcpServers` for (Claude, Codex, muse). HRC changes nothing for provisioning. An agent whose spaces exclude the space keeps the CLIs; that is the migration unit (S1, gate 5).
- **Humans keep the CLIs.** `wrkq`, `wrkc` and `hrc` remain for Lance and for the transition. Whether a human-facing `praesidium` CLI replaces them later is open (§7) and out of this record.

### 2.7 Degradation and persistence

Unchanged from the record §3.8–3.9 and D17: persisted snapshot, resume at last-good, stale-never-absent, `feed` node per binding, `praesidium kernel rebuild` discards the cache. Two additions for agents: a **read tool answers from a stale snapshot and says so** (the result carries the subject's `feed` state and `cursorAt`), and a **write tool to an unreachable owner refuses `owner_unreachable {owner, since}`** rather than queueing, because the owner's fence cannot be supplied from a snapshot and a queued write would be the kernel acting later with authority it does not hold (record §3.8 rule 3).

### 2.8 Multi-node

One kernel per node. Each binds every HRC node's page, so an agent on svc sees max3's seats, with `node` on each. Each node's wrkq binding reads the one canonical wrkqd on mini, so every node sees the same tasks, rooms and extensions (r1.1, F3: nothing shared is node-local). Agents use their node's kernel. The `hrc@<node>` binding for the local node goes over the same HTTP routes as the remote ones, on loopback.

## 3. Rules: the strangler contract

- **S1 Surfaces are replaced.** Every CLI form an agent uses gets an operation, a parity-table row, and a generated tool. Migration is per space membership. The injected guides retire per migrated verb. Fully committed.
- **S2 Stores move one subject at a time, by ruling.** A subject's identity, state, obligation or ledger register leaves its owner only when the kernel reproduces its full lifecycle (fences, sweeps, obligation discharge, federation routing), gates it, and **deletes the old path in the same campaign**. No deferred retirement. Merging daemons or stores wholesale remains unsupported (record §6, D1).
- **S3 Projection tax.** Once a register is read through the kernel, an owner change that the kernel does not project is incomplete. Each owner's definition of done gains "projected in the praesidium kernel or declared human-only".
- **S4 Dependency direction.** HRC, wrkq and aspd never call the kernel. Handoff stays in HRC (rotation injects it; record M1). The kernel is a client of all three.
- **S5 A new register lives where every one of its consumers can reach it.** In revision one that is the system that mints its subject (record R1), because the kernel has no shared store and a node-local one would partition writers from consumers on other nodes (F3). The kernel becomes a candidate home for new registers only under a shared-store design of its own (§6), which this record does not propose. The r1 claim that owners stop growing from phase 4 is withdrawn.

## 5. Decisions

| id | decision | why |
|---|---|---|
| K1 | A separate kernel process per node, not a document inside rusthrc. | The surface every agent runs through must not share a failure domain, restart authority or release cadence with the daemon that births and rotates agents; it is the idiomatic cardo shape; it inherits none of rusthrc's eight single-document assumptions; max3 stops being special. |
| K2 | The operation catalog is the standard; the document is a cache in front of it. | Agents write, read history and bodies, and wait. R3 keeps those out of the document and that is right; delegated read-through ops serve them under the owner's own sweeps. |
| K3 | Caller identity is bound per connection from HRC's environment, including the daemon-minted lifecycle credential, checked against the live seat table, and forwarded verbatim to every owner; HRC verifies the credential as it does for the CLI. | Parity with today's trust plus one check the CLIs never make; every ledger row keeps naming the agent; `seat.restart` and the lifecycle route keep their credential admission with no HRC change (F1). |
| K4 | Tools are generated from discovery and served by an MCP bridge that agent-spaces provisions as a space. | cardo already generates tools from declarations; raspd already composes `mcpServers`; space membership is a per-agent migration switch with trivial rollback. |
| K5 | Writes go through `agent.sock` with durable admission and outcome journaling; reads through `kernel.sock`. **The kernel never repeats a write.** An uncertain outcome is reported as uncertain for every class; the class tells the agent what a same-key or same-fence repeat means under the owner's own contract. | The kernel cannot close an owner's transaction boundary (wrkq's post-commit replay insert; HRC's fenceless action append), so it claims nothing about duplicates beyond what the owner records (F2, two cycles). An uncertain outcome is what the CLI gives today. |
| K6 | A write to an unreachable owner refuses; it is never queued. | The kernel holds no authority of its own; a queued write would execute later with a fence the caller never supplied. |

## 6. Explicitly unsupported

Merging daemons or stores; the kernel holding write authority, fences or a store of its own; the kernel verifying or minting lifecycle credentials (HRC does); queuing writes for an unreachable owner; the kernel repeating any write on any outcome; closing an owner's own replay window from the kernel (wrkq's replay row is inserted after the effect commits; a wrkqd interruption inside that window is the owner's defect, reachable from the CLI today, and not gated here); the kernel as a dependency of HRC, wrkq or aspd; replacing `wrkf.*`, webhooks, index or daemon-lifecycle surfaces; a human `praesidium` CLI; a node-local register for any shared subject; inferring the caller from anything but the provisioned environment; projecting ledgers, closed tasks, terminal envelopes or bodies into the document (they are read-through ops); hierarchies while the `hrc` document is live; seat records (`asks`, `work`, `kind`, `handoff`) anywhere but HRC; remote send, summon or rotation beyond what the home already allows.

## 10. Sizing, honestly

This is the largest re-platforming praesidium has attempted. Phases 1–4 are each a campaign of several task-scoped seats over days, not hours; phase 5 is a long tail across 154 files that runs while everything else proceeds. Two surfaces coexist for the whole of phase 5. The kernel becomes the critical path for every migrated agent write, where today only Loggia depends on cardo.
